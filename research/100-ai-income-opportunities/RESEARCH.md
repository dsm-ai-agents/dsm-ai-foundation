# Research findings

**Snapshot: 7 November 2026.** All recommendations below are research-informed judgments. Exact offers and prices remain hypotheses.

## Main conclusion

The most defensible starting point is a narrow service built around knowledge the professional already has: cleaning source information, preparing a useful deliverable, checking quality and fitting the work into a customer's existing process. A reusable product or plugin becomes more attractive after several customers demonstrate the same need.

This is a recommendation, not a demonstrated universal sequence. A professional with an existing audience may reasonably test a digital product first; an experienced developer with distribution may test software first.

## What the evidence supports

| Finding | Evidence | Commercial implication | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| US small businesses are adopting AI, but adoption estimates vary greatly | Chamber 2026 and NFIB 2025 [S01](SOURCES.md#s01), [S02](SOURCES.md#s02) | Training and implementation deserve testing | Different populations, dates and definitions; do not average their percentages |
| Some AI-related freelance work has actual completed-job earnings | Upwork's US-origin marketplace analysis [S03](SOURCES.md#s03) | Integration, creative production and operational work are credible categories to investigate | Aggregate platform demand does not establish beginner earnings |
| Businesses pay for AI tools, with adoption varying across firms | Chase transaction research [S04](SOURCES.md#s04) | Seek firms with a defined workflow and existing software budget | Tool spending is not proof of willingness to hire a consultant |
| A basic generation feature can be free within an existing subscription | Shopify Magic [S11](SOURCES.md#s11) | Sell catalog accuracy, completeness and integration, not generic copy alone | The value of a premium offer still needs a sales test |
| Teams struggle to convert individual tool use into coordinated work | Thomson Reuters and Mollick's LinkedIn discussion [S06](SOURCES.md#s06), [S19](SOURCES.md#s19) | Shared procedures, knowledge organization and training are plausible offers | Global survey and expert opinion are not identical evidence |
| Service delivery and acquisition remain difficult | Two Reddit discussions [S17](SOURCES.md#s17), [S18](SOURCES.md#s18) | Include maintenance, human review and a first-customer strategy | Anecdotal; not a representative survey |
| Platforms evolve and can invalidate a product premise | Current OpenAI plugin and migration documentation [S15](SOURCES.md#s15), [S16](SOURCES.md#s16) | Test permissions, distribution and functionality before selling | Account-specific availability; no assumed marketplace payout |

The Chamber reports 66% AI use in its 2026 study; NFIB reported 24% in its 2025 member survey. These do not form a clean time series. The meaningful finding is variation, not a manufactured single adoption rate. Correlations between adoption and business growth also do not prove AI caused the growth.

## Competing alternatives and differentiation

**Digital downloads:** Free tutorials and general-purpose models can replicate generic material. Differentiate through a specific profession, tested examples, editable assets and a clear outcome. A large catalog alone provides little advantage.

**Content services:** Buyers can generate drafts themselves. The service must add interviews, original evidence, editorial judgment, consistent delivery or specialist checking. Claims about guaranteed SEO rankings or viral reach are excluded.

**Workflow automation:** Existing software often already has reminders, templates and integrations. Compare a conventional automation with an AI version. Use AI only where interpreting messy inputs or drafting language adds value.

**Customer-support tools:** Intercom advertises usage-based AI support, including a starting $0.99 per outcome price [S13](SOURCES.md#s13). That is an underlying software comparator, not a freelancer rate. A service must add knowledge cleanup, evaluation, integration or ongoing quality management.

**Plugins:** Packaging instructions is accessible, but installing, sharing and integrating them may be restricted. The current OpenAI documentation supports plugin creation and migration workflows; it does not establish that listing a plugin will generate revenue. Prefer clearly priced customization, maintenance or an independently billable service where allowed [S15](SOURCES.md#s15), [S16](SOURCES.md#s16).

## What we deliberately did not claim

- That all 100 ideas already earn money, or that any working professional can deliver each one.
- That social engagement, an influencer's audience or a screenshot proves sales.
- That broad enterprise adoption proves small businesses can pay similar fees.
- That a fixed-price project constitutes monthly recurring revenue.
- That recovered staff time becomes cash savings automatically.
- That building an AI wrapper creates a durable competitive advantage.

## What would change the recommendations

Promote an idea after a real buyer pays, uses the deliverable and asks to repeat or expand it at acceptable delivery cost. Downgrade it when an existing feature solves the problem, data access is impractical, corrections dominate delivery, no budget owner emerges, or the offer requires professional authority the seller lacks.

A stronger next research round should collect direct buyer evidence in hospitality, manufacturing, logistics and agriculture, plus current competing service quotes for the five shortlisted offers. This pass provides breadth and a documented starting set; it does not substitute for those tests.
