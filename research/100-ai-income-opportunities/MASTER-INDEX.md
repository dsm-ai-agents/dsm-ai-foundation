# Master index: assess, choose, test

Start with the work you already understand. Choose one customer problem, then test whether someone will pay for a small solution.

**Navigation:** Master index → [chapter index](CHAPTER-INDEX.md) → chapter → topic → paid validation.

## 1. Assess your starting point

Answer these questions before browsing ideas:

1. What work can I already do well enough to check AI output?
2. Which buyers can I reach through real professional relationships?
3. How many hours can I spend each week, including selling and support?
4. Do I prefer delivering a service, teaching, selling a reusable product, or maintaining software?
5. Can I demonstrate this safely with public, synthetic or authorized data?

If you cannot check the output or reach a buyer, start with learning and buyer conversations before building.

## 3. Assess how you want to earn

| Preference | Consider | Reality check |
|---|---|---|
| A small first engagement | A fixed-scope service such as AI-011 or AI-016 | You need a fresh sale for each project |
| Repeat client work | Retainers such as AI-006 or AI-026 | Ongoing work, support and retention are required |
| Reusable products | Downloads such as AI-001, AI-025 or AI-074 | You need distribution; low build cost does not create demand |
| Teaching | AI-020, AI-040 or AI-046 | Expertise, preparation and a way to assess learning matter |
| Software subscriptions | AI-050 or AI-100 | Support, reliability, acquisition and churn continue after launch |

## 4. Decide what to test

Shortlist three topics. Rate each **strong / uncertain / weak** on existing skills, buyer access, problem frequency, budget, delivery capacity and ability to verify quality. These are judgments, not a statistical score. Reject topics you cannot deliver responsibly. Prefer the one with the clearest buyer and smallest useful paid pilot.

Before committing: compare an existing free or bundled tool; ask a buyer about the last time the problem occurred; make one small sample; request a paid pilot. If nobody pays, revise the offer before building more.

## 5. Check the money and next action

Read [economics and capacity](ECONOMICS.md). All catalog prices are test hypotheses. $1,000–$10,000 refers to illustrative monthly gross revenue, not guaranteed earnings or take-home pay.

Then follow the [30-day validation plan](PLAN.md). Begin with one idea, not all 100.
