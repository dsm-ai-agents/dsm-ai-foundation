# Methodology and research log

Research conducted **7 November 2026**. Target market: US buyers. Research includes global sources where explicitly labeled.

## Questions

- What work are organizations using AI for, and what do they pay people to do?
- Which problems can a working professional solve with existing expertise?
- What competes with the proposed offer, including bundled/free features?
- What additional human judgment or implementation makes the offer worth buying?
- What would have to be true to reach $1,000, $5,000 or $10,000 gross monthly?
- What public counterevidence challenges easy-income narratives?

## Method

1. Inspect the repository and existing project instructions.
2. Search primary industry surveys, observed marketplace work and platform documentation.
3. Search Reddit, LinkedIn and X for first-person experiences and credible practitioners.
4. Open key supporting sources, retain access status and distinguish indexed snippets.
5. Synthesize business models into 20 groups with five distinct deliverables each.
6. Label prices, hours, acquisition routes and revenue scenarios as author proposals.
7. Prioritize a small shortlist using buyer clarity, relevant evidence, inspectable outputs and entry effort.
8. Save narrative findings plus structured source and idea records.

No subagents were used. No interviews, outreach, purchases, private-account access or revenue verification were performed. We did not conduct an exhaustive systematic review or representative social-media survey.

## Evidence classes

- **Primary research:** industry/association surveys, transaction analysis and marketplace completed-job data.
- **Platform facts:** features, documentation and published software/platform prices.
- **Named implementation:** a described real organization using a tool; sponsorship noted.
- **Practitioner interpretation:** identifiable work and expert opinion; not income proof.
- **Public anecdote:** self-reported experiences and objections.
- **Discovery-only:** search excerpts, inaccessible pages or unverified promotional assertions.

Evidence confidence is about the source's relevance to a workflow. It is separate from commercial attractiveness and from the probability that an individual succeeds. No idea is labeled a proven revenue opportunity.

## Selection and exclusions

Prefer original institutions and vendor pages for the facts they can establish. Preserve survey geography, underlying data dates, sponsorship and sample limitations. Search snippets alone are insufficient for numerical claims in the main conclusions.

Exclude repeated syndications as independent corroboration. Treat revenue posts as unverified unless independent records substantiate them. Exclude automated social summaries and course-marketing claims from income evidence.

## Known limitations and next research

The source collection has stronger coverage of general SMB adoption, ecommerce, professional services, construction, restaurants and nonprofit administration than of hospitality, manufacturing, logistics and agriculture. Even strong sector adoption does not verify one proposed offer.

No comprehensive service-rate comparison was completed. Published platform fees are included only as cost/substitute references. All offer prices require buyer testing and scoped competitor comparison.

Exact social-post dates were not always exposed. X direct retrieval returned 403 for checked examples. Publicly indexed excerpts were not treated as full-thread inspection. The report cannot establish the volume or representativeness of platform chatter.

Before images or publication, refresh facts that depend on platform rules, prices or program deadlines. Before implementation, validate buyer need, seller competence, data permissions and the actual delivery process.
