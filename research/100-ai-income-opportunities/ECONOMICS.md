# Revenue, costs and delivery capacity

All offer prices and workload estimates in this project are **proposed assumptions**, not sourced market rates. The $1,000–$10,000 goal is **monthly gross revenue**, not take-home pay. No time-to-income prediction is made.

## Equations

- Gross monthly revenue = units sold that month × price per unit.
- Retainer/subscription revenue = active paying customers × monthly price.
- Contribution before owner labor, tax and overhead = revenue − platform fees − direct software/delivery expenses − acquisition expenses − refunds.
- Effective pre-tax hourly return = contribution ÷ all owner hours, including selling, learning, setup, revisions and administration.
- Delivery capacity = available hours after non-delivery work ÷ hours per unit.

A project sold once does not recur automatically. Downloads require fresh buyers each month; subscriptions require retention. No conversion or churn rate has been assumed as a fact.

## Three illustrative business cases

### 1. A $49 digital kit: AI-001

- 21 sales produce $1,029 gross; 103 produce $5,047; 205 produce $10,045.
- Under Gumroad's published direct-sale fee of 10% + $0.50, 103 sales incur $556.20 in platform fees, leaving $4,490.80 before other expenses [S12](SOURCES.md#s12).
- With an assumed $300 in tools/design and $500 acquisition spend, contribution is $3,690.80 before owner labor, tax and overhead.
- The catalog conservatively budgets one support/fulfillment hour per sale: 103 hours at this volume. Adding 20 build/update hours and 20 selling/admin hours gives 143 owner hours; implied pre-tax return is about $25.81/hour.
- This may be too support-heavy. Test whether a better product reduces support to 15 minutes per buyer; do not simply assume it will.
- Discover-sourced sales have a different fee. At 30%, the same $5,047 leaves $3,532.90 before other costs. Distribution terms materially affect economics.
- Sales can be zero. The revenue arithmetic does not establish an audience or acquisition cost.

### 2. A $500 content retainer: AI-006

- Two clients produce $1,000; ten produce $5,000; twenty produce $10,000.
- At six delivery hours/client, ten clients require 60 hours/month. Add an assumed 20 sales/admin hours and ten revision/onboarding hours: 90 total.
- Assume $150 tools, $150 payment costs and $400 acquisition expense. Contribution on $5,000 is $4,300 before owner labor, tax and overhead, or about $47.78 per owner hour.
- At twenty clients, delivery alone requires 120 hours. A professional with ten hours/week cannot serve that workload without materially changing scope, pricing or staffing.
- Retention, interviews completed on time and revision limits decide whether the model works.

### 3. A $2,500 migration project: AI-096

- One project produces $2,500; two produce $5,000; four produce $10,000.
- At 30 delivery hours/project, two projects require 60 delivery hours. Add an assumed 20 sales/scoping hours and ten support/admin hours: 90 total.
- Assume $300 test tooling/hosting and $500 combined acquisition, payment and specialist expenses. Contribution on $5,000 is $4,200 before owner labor, tax and overhead, or about $46.67/hour.
- Four projects require 120 delivery hours before any non-delivery time. Rebuilt integrations can exceed the assumed scope.
- Maintenance must be separately scoped; a migration fee is not recurring revenue.

Only the cited platform fee is sourced. All remaining expense and time inputs are examples to replace with actual records.

## Working-professional capacity

A planning budget of ten hours/week is roughly 43 hours/month. Reserving 15 for selling, learning and administration leaves about 28 delivery hours. At the catalog's assumptions this supports approximately:

| Offer | Units fitting 28 delivery hours | Gross revenue from those units | Important exclusion |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| AI-006 content retainer | 4 clients | $2,000 | Onboarding and revisions can reduce capacity |
| AI-011 catalog cleanup | 2 projects | $1,500 | Requires two fresh sales each month |
| AI-046 workshop | 1 workshop | $1,500 | Preparation may repeat for each audience |
| AI-096 migration | 0 full 30-hour projects within one month | $0 completed-project revenue under this strict budget | Could span months; do not mislabel booked work as earned monthly income |

The route to $10,000 often requires more hours, higher-value scope, reusable delivery, an existing audience, or a team. It should not be marketed as a universal side-hustle outcome.

## Pricing validation

Before publishing an offer, obtain at least three relevant competing quotes or clearly scoped public prices, identify the budget owner, and ask for a paid pilot. Keep software subscription prices separate from professional-service prices. Measure actual hours and correction rates, then set revision limits, usage caps and support boundaries.

Do not infer price from a claimed percentage of buyer savings. A saved hour has value only if the buyer can productively redeploy it or avoid a real cost.
