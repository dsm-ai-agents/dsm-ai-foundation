# From 100 ideas to validated offers and images

This plan separates the completed research/catalog phase from future buyer validation and visual production. No outreach or product launch has been performed. Image production was subsequently authorized and completed; see the execution update below.

## Working framework

**Know → Solve → Package → Prove → Repeat**

1. **Know:** Start with a professional capability the reader can demonstrate.
2. **Solve:** Select a recurring problem with an identifiable budget owner.
3. **Package:** Define the deliverable, exclusions, price hypothesis and delivery process.
4. **Prove:** Test with real inputs and a paying customer; measure corrections and usefulness.
5. **Repeat:** Improve repeatability before expanding the product or producing a large campaign.

The 100 ideas are a portfolio of options, not a recommendation to build 100 businesses.

## Proposed 30-day validation cycle

These are decision thresholds for an experiment, not predicted results.

### Days 1–7: Choose and understand

Pick at most two offers based on existing skills and buyer access. Review three competing solutions per offer. Conduct five permission-based buyer conversations per offer. Ask about the last occurrence of the problem, frequency, current workaround, consequences, who approves spending and what they have already tried.

**Proceed:** at least three buyers describe a recent concrete problem and at least one identifiable budget owner agrees to inspect a sample.  
**Change direction:** the problem is rare, already solved cheaply, or the proposed seller cannot access required data.

### Days 8–14: Prepare proof and request payment

Build a small sample using synthetic, public or expressly authorized inputs. Define a fixed pilot scope, price, revision limit, required inputs and acceptance criteria. Offer it to the buyers who expressed interest. Do not count compliments, waitlist signups or social likes as sales.

**Proceed:** at least one paid pilot at a price worth testing.  
**Stop or revise:** no willingness to pay after the initial conversations plus five additional qualified conversations; diagnose offer, buyer or trust problems before building more.

### Days 15–21: Deliver and measure

Record every hour and software expense. Track substantive corrections, missing information, usage and the buyer's observed outcome. For generated factual materials, every material claim must be checked. For integrations, run a shadow pilot and verify access boundaries, failures and escalation before production.

**Proceed:** buyer accepts the deliverable and the actual contribution per owner hour meets the seller's chosen minimum.  
**Re-scope:** review work or exceptions erase the margin.

### Days 22–30: Decide what deserves repetition

Ask whether the buyer wants another purchase, a renewal or a referral. Repeat with a second independent buyer. Update the idea record with measured price, hours and outcomes. Keep failures in the record.

**Promote to validated:** two independent paid buyers, accepted deliverables, and at least one repeat purchase or paid renewal. This is an internal editorial threshold, not proof of broad market demand.  
**Retain as hypothesis:** promising feedback without payment or repeat use.  
**Archive:** repeated lack of budget or unacceptable delivery risk/cost.

## Idea development workflow

For each idea selected for expansion:

1. Confirm source claims and publication dates.
2. Write a one-page offer: buyer, trigger, deliverable, exclusions, inputs, price and first-customer route.
3. Add a real or clearly labeled synthetic before/after example.
4. Check economic arithmetic and capacity.
5. Obtain domain review where needed.
6. Freeze an editorial version before creating images.
7. Update the CSV/JSON and readable card together; keep the stable AI-### ID.

## Original image production recommendation

Start with five prototype cards from the shortlist and one collection cover. Use the [visual brief](VISUAL-BRIEF.md). Review readability, evidence labels and layout together before producing larger batches.

Suggested structure for the eventual collection: one overview, 20 industry dividers and 100 idea cards. Optional expanded carousels can be planned later; no social platform or publication cadence is assumed.

Every visual should carry the idea ID, buyer, problem, offer, entry level, price hypothesis, one simple revenue example and a concise constraint. Include a source footer or link to its research card. Never show a hypothetical number as earned income.
